🕹️ What Is time-crisis-2-vr?

time-crisis-2-vr is a virtual reality version of the classic arcade light-gun shooter Time Crisis II. Instead of standing at a cabinet and pointing a plastic pistol at a screen, you put on a VR headset and step directly into the action. You aim with your own hands, duck behind cover, and pop out to take your shot.

The project is a native port, which means it was built to run on VR hardware from the ground up rather than being wrapped around an old screen-based version. It supports two ways to play: standalone on a Meta Quest 3 headset, and on a Windows computer connected to a PCVR headset. Both routes use OpenXR, the modern standard for VR software, so your headset and controllers are recognized automatically.

The foundation of the project is spacestate1/namco22-decompile, a decompilation effort that reconstructs the original Namco System 22 arcade hardware behavior in readable, modifiable code. That foundation is what allows the game logic to run natively in a modern VR environment while preserving the timing, feel, and enemy behavior of the arcade original.

✨ Key Features

  • True VR aiming. Point your controller where you want to shoot. No cursor, no reticle drift — just your hands and your aim.
  • Cover-based arcade combat. Step behind objects to reload and stay alive, then lean out to return fire, just like the arcade cabinet's foot pedal mechanic.
  • Two ways to play. Run it standalone on Meta Quest 3 or through a Windows PC connected to a PCVR headset.
  • OpenXR support. Works with the standard VR runtime, which means fewer compatibility headaches and broader headset support.
  • Faithful arcade pacing. Enemy waves, timers, and stage flow follow the original design rather than a modern reinterpretation.
  • Open and inspectable. Because it is built on a decompilation project, the code is readable and the community can study, fix, and improve it.
  • Actively maintained repository. Updates, fixes, and build notes are published on the project page.

🎯 How to Play

The core loop is simple and satisfying. You move through a stage, enemies appear from cover, and you have a short window to react before they fire. Aim with your dominant hand controller and press the trigger to shoot.

The defining mechanic is the cover system. When you are not shooting, step behind a wall, crate, or pillar. Behind cover you are safe, and this is also when you reload. Step back out when you are ready and take your next shot. Because you are physically moving in your play space, you control the rhythm of the fight with your own body.

Give yourself a clear area of about two meters by two meters. Stand with your feet apart, keep your cable-tethered headset from tangling, and make sure nothing breakable is within arm's reach.

🔧 Troubleshooting Tips

  • The headset is not detected. Close the application, confirm your headset software is running, then start the application again.
  • The view looks wrong or off-center. Re-center your view using the reset or recenter option in your headset's own menu.
  • The game stutters. Lower your graphics settings, close background apps, and make sure your graphics drivers are current.
  • Controllers do not respond. Check the batteries or charge level, then re-pair them through your headset settings.
  • Windows blocks the file. If a security prompt appears, choose the option to keep the file only if you downloaded it from the official project page linked above.
  • Nothing happens after launch. Right-click the application and choose "Run as administrator" to rule out permission problems.
  • Audio comes out of the wrong device. Open Windows Sound settings and set your headset as the default playback device.

🛠️ Built on a Decompiled Arcade Foundation

The original Time Crisis II ran on Namco System 22 arcade hardware, a platform with very specific timing and rendering behavior. The spacestate1/namco22-decompile project reconstructs that platform in portable source code. By building on it, time-crisis-2-vr can reproduce the original logic without needing emulation of the entire arcade board.

The practical benefit for you is stability and authenticity. The pacing, enemy timing, and stage structure come from the original design, while the presentation is rebuilt for stereo rendering and hand-tracked aiming. You do not need to know anything about the internals to play, but if you are curious, the source is open and readable.
